## Cheapest design that still works: 26.49 USD (10 pct less)

The search covered 96 combinations: boom material (cardboard, pine, plywood, PLA+) x tube (soft PVC, PU 98A, nylon-12, nylon + PU loops) x joint syringes (20|20, 10|20, 10|10) x gripper syringes (5|5, 10|10). 33 of them pass the working bars: 2b (stroke chains), 4 (torque margin 1.5 with an empty can), 5 (grip) and 6 (placement within the stack tolerance, no air).
The cheapest that works:
- **Boom:** plywood (it is also the only material that passes the full-can stretch bar 8).
- **Tube:** polyurethane 98A all the way.
- **Shoulder and elbow:** a 10 mL master on a 20 mL slave (1.74x force; the thumb pushes 1.74x further, still inside the master's stroke).
- **Gripper:** 5|5.

What sets the price floor:
- **Soft PVC aquarium tube** is the cheapest tube (0.30 USD/m), but every PVC option fails bar 6. Its wall stretches about 5x more than PU, so 2 x the placement error is 49-108 mm against the 16.9 mm the stack tolerates. This is the main real failure of syringe arms, and the bar catches it (planted control in bar 13).
- **The three big fixed items** are the plywood base (7.00), the printed parts (7.02) and the tube (about 5-7). Where to cut next, not yet in the search: a cardboard or MDF-offcut base, printed parts at 15 pct infill, and shorter tube runs (the console moved closer to the base).
