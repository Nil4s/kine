# CH1a feedback: building a framed one-storey house in Kine

What was done: reference (docs/CH1_REFERENCE.md), a 578-member Part record (data/challenges/ch1_house.json, generated by tools/ch1_build_house.py), a Building record for the blueprint, plan + 4 elevations + 1 section, a making-of video, out/ch1/members.json for CH1b. Test tests/ch1a_test.gd: 16 of 16 bars.
Honest limits: the room sizes are my reconstruction (the Levitt drawing could not be read as text), so the 2 % bars hold the replay to my own numbers, measured from the replayed plates, not to a surveyed drawing. The roof pitch (4:12) and framing sizes are IRC practice, not read off the original.

Ranked by difficulty to fix. Each item is something that was hard or impossible.

## Easy

1. **Per-body bounds are not in the replay result.** To find where a body is, I had to read the mesh vertices through `surf_ranges`. A `body_boxes` array (AABB per body, mm) in `Part.replay` would remove 30 lines from every test and tool. The data is already there when the mesh is built.
2. **More than 256 bodies: bodies are merged by look and the look includes the body NAME.** A 578-body house gave one distinct look per named body, so the last ~320 bodies all took the look of the last surface (shingles, siding and windows came out stud-coloured; found by looking at the render, no warning shown). Fix: leave the name out of the merge key (or merge by colour and material only), and warn in the result when the cap is hit. Workaround used: name bodies by kind ("Stud", "Header"), unique names kept in my own member list.
3. **Blueprint title block says "Front elevation" on any custom sheet**, and the Back and Left views are not tabs. I made them by turning the room 180 degrees; the title cell then says Front/Side. Add Back and Left tabs and let `Blueprint.sheet` take a title.
4. **The plan has no room names or room areas.** Rooms are only the space between walls. A "label" on an interior-wall pair, or `rooms` with a name and a clear-size note, would turn the plan into a real house plan.
5. **The plan does not show stud spacing or the framing.** It draws walls as one black polygon. A "framing" view that draws plates and studs (from the same record) is the simplest way to show the 16 in grid on the paper.

## Medium

6. **Interior walls always run the full width or depth of the room.** A partition that stops at another partition (a T) cannot be drawn, so the plan became a 3 x 2 grid of rooms. Give an interior wall `from` and `to`, as exterior walls already have `a0` and `a1`.
7. **No framing piece kinds.** A stud wall is: bottom plate, studs on a grid, openings with king, jack, header, sill and cripples, double top plate. I generated that by script (578 boxes). A "Stud wall" command (length, height, grid, openings list) in Build that makes those members and names them would turn a 2-hour job into a minute and makes the member list free. Same for a rafter set (span, pitch, spacing) and a joist set.
8. **A sloped member has to be a sketch plus an extrude (3 features) in a hand-made plane.** The right plane's sketch x runs along minus Z, which cost a probe to find. An "angled box" prim (a box with a rotation about one axis) or a "rafter" piece kind (run, rise, depth, birdsmouth) would make roofs one feature each.
9. **Roof pieces with a pattern need the source built first and indexed by body number.** I tracked body counts myself so the appear and pattern steps pointed at the right body. A name instead of an index in `appear` and `pattern` ("body": "Rafter front") would remove that whole class of mistakes.
10. **The film path is a script I wrote.** Making-of video = replay a prefix of the steps, orbit the camera, ffmpeg. The app has Render shots and Film keys but no "build film" that reveals a Part's steps one by one with a label per stage. The step list already has the order and names; marking stage boundaries (I added a `stages` list) and a "Render making-of" button would do it.
11. **Section drawings do not exist.** Plan and elevations come from the Building record; a section cut through a Part (a plane position, members cut in black, others outline) had to be drawn by hand from my member list. A `section_sheet(part, plane)` in Blueprint is the missing piece.
12. **Building and Part are two separate records.** The blueprint reads Building (rooms, walls, openings); the framing is a Part. They share nothing, so room sizes and openings are typed twice and could drift. A test (this one) is the only link. Generating the Part framing from the Building record would keep one source.

## Hard

13. **No structural check in the app.** The brief asks that the house does not fall down. Kine can check that members touch (this test does) but not loads, spans, bearing or uplift: no rule that a header of this size carries this opening, that joists fit the span table, that a bearing line has a path to the footing, that rafter thrust is held by the ties. CH1b's `house_check.gd` is the first piece. A member list with nominal size, span, spacing and support (out/ch1/members.json) is the input format I would keep.
14. **No wall assembly or notching.** Real framing has lapped corners, birdsmouth cuts, notched joists at a rafter, hangers and nails. Here each member is a separate body that touches or overlaps its neighbour (rafter heels overlap the joists where both fall on the same x, every 1219 mm; the test counts touching, not overlap). A "join with notch" operation (cut a member by another, keep both) is a boolean per pair and gets slow at 600 bodies.
15. **Size.** 578 bodies replay in 1.4 s, fine, but the surface cap, one pick per body and the step list of 892 steps are at the edge of what the UI handles. Instancing (the replay has `instances`) for the 200 identical studs would keep the step list readable and fix item 2 at the root.
